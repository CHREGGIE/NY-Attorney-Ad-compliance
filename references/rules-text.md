## 1. Rule 7.1: Communications Concerning a Lawyer's Services

> (a) A lawyer shall not make a false or misleading communication about the lawyer or the lawyer's services. A communication is false or misleading if it contains a material misrepresentation of fact or law, or omits a fact necessary to make the statement considered as a whole not materially misleading.
>
> (b) A lawyer may communicate information regarding the lawyer's services through any media.
>
> (c) A lawyer shall not state or imply that a lawyer is certified as a specialist in a particular field of law, unless:
> (1) the lawyer has been certified as a specialist by an organization that has been approved by an appropriate authority of the state or the District of Columbia or a U.S. Territory or that has been accredited by the American Bar Association; and
> (2) the name of the certifying organization is clearly identified in the communication.
>
> (d) Any communication made under this Rule must include the name and contact information of at least one lawyer or law firm responsible for its content.

**Comment summaries (NYSBA, as amended 2026; largely adopted from ABA Model Rule 7.1 comments):**
- **Cmt [1]:** The rule covers *all* communications about the lawyer or the lawyer's services, including advertising and communications to existing clients and other lawyers. (The old rule exempted existing clients and other lawyers. It no longer does.)
- **ABA-derived comments on misleading-but-true statements:** A truthful statement can be misleading if it omits necessary context, or if there's a substantial likelihood it leads a reasonable person to an unwarranted conclusion about the lawyer or the services. Truthful reports of past results can be misleading if they create an unjustified expectation that similar results will follow regardless of the facts. Unsubstantiated comparisons with other lawyers' services can be misleading. A disclaimer or qualifying language may prevent a statement from being misleading. Statements implying an ability to influence a government agency or official improperly, or to get results by violating the law, are prohibited.
- **Cmt [7]:** A lawyer may generally say they "concentrate in", are a "specialist", practice a "specialty", or "specialize in" particular fields based on experience, training, or education. These claims are still subject to the false-or-misleading standard. Only a claim of **certification** triggers 7.1(c).
- **Cmt [10]:** Contact information should always include a **telephone number or a physical office address**, and may also include a website, email address, and other contact info.

**What the June 2026 rewrite removed** (see `obsolete-rules.md`): the "Attorney Advertising" label, the mandatory "Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome" disclaimer, the principal-office-address requirement, retention and archiving, pop-up and meta-tag rules, the actors/portrayal rules, the legal-document look-alike rule, and the fee-advertising mechanics. All of these now fall under the general false-or-misleading standard (and Rule 8.4(c)).

## 2. Rule 7.2: Payment for Referrals (unchanged by the 2026 amendments)

> (a) A lawyer shall not compensate or give anything of value to a person or organization to recommend or obtain employment by a client, or as a reward for having made a recommendation resulting in employment by a client, except that:
> (1) a lawyer or law firm may refer clients to a nonlegal professional or nonlegal professional service firm pursuant to a contractual relationship with such nonlegal professional or nonlegal professional service firm to provide legal and other professional services on a systematic and continuing basis as permitted by Rule 5.8, provided however that such referral shall not otherwise include any monetary or other tangible consideration or reward for such, or the sharing of legal fees; and
> (2) a lawyer may pay the usual and reasonable fees or dues charged by a qualified legal assistance organization or referral fees to another lawyer as permitted by Rule 1.5(g).

Paragraph (b) addresses legal aid offices, public defenders, military legal assistance, bar-sponsored referral services, and similar qualified legal assistance organizations. It rarely matters for private-firm marketing.

**Practical reading:** paying for advertising, directory listings, SEO, and marketing services is payment for media, not for a recommendation, and is generally fine. Fee-sharing with another lawyer goes through Rule 1.5(g) (proportional work or joint responsibility, client consent confirmed in writing, total fee not excessive). Risk areas: pay-per-lead or pay-per-signed-client vendors that "recommend" the lawyer, gifts or rewards to clients or contacts tied to referrals, and "refer a friend" incentives.

## 3. Rule 7.3: Solicitation of Clients

> (a) "Solicitation" or "solicit" denotes a communication initiated by or on behalf of a lawyer or law firm that is directed to a specific person the lawyer knows or reasonably should know needs legal services in a particular matter and that offers to provide, or reasonably can be understood as offering to provide, legal services for that matter.
>
> (b) A lawyer shall not solicit professional employment by live person-to-person contact when a significant motive for the lawyer's doing so is the lawyer's or law firm's pecuniary gain, unless the contact is with a:
> (1) a lawyer;
> (2) a person who has a family, close personal, or prior business or professional relationship with the lawyer or law firm; or
> (3) a person who routinely uses for business purposes the type of legal services offered by the lawyer.
>
> (c) A lawyer shall not solicit professional employment even when not otherwise prohibited by paragraph (a), if:
> (1) the target of the solicitation has made known to the lawyer a desire not to be solicited by the lawyer; or
> (2) the solicitation involves coercion, duress or harassment.
>
> (d) This Rule does not prohibit communications authorized by law or ordered by a court or other tribunal.
>
> (e) Notwithstanding the prohibitions in this Rule, a lawyer may participate with a prepaid or group legal service plan operated by an organization not owned or directed by the lawyer that uses live person-to-person contact to enroll members or sell subscriptions for the plan from persons who are not known to need legal services in a particular matter covered by the plan.

**Comment summaries:**
- General advertising to the public, and communications responding to a request for information, are not "solicitation" (they aren't directed at a specific person known to need services in a particular matter).
- "Live person-to-person" means in-person, face-to-face, live phone, and real-time visual or auditory contact. Chat, text, email, and social DMs that the recipient can ignore are generally treated as written, not live. Under the ABA comments, those are outside 7.3(b) but still subject to 7.1 and 7.3(c).
- **Cmt [5]:** There's less risk of overreaching when the person contacted routinely uses the type of legal services involved for business purposes. NYSBA gives these examples: people who routinely hire outside counsel for an entity; entrepreneurs who regularly engage business, employment, or IP lawyers; small business proprietors who routinely hire lawyers for lease or contract issues. This does **not** cover every business owner or every field of law.

**Removed in 2026:** filing solicitations with the disciplinary committee, keeping recipient lists, the 30-day bar on PI/wrongful-death solicitation, "SAMPLE / DO NOT SIGN" retainer marking, and the old written-solicitation mechanics. Federal statutes on aviation and rail disasters (49 U.S.C. §§1136, 1139) and Judiciary Law §480 (hospital entry) are unaffected. Rule 4.5 (defense-side contact after PI incidents) is a separate rule. Check its current text if auditing PI or insurance-defense marketing.

## 5. Rule 7.5: Professional Notices, Letterheads and Names (amended June 24, 2020; retained in 2026 even though the ABA dropped its equivalent)

> (a) A lawyer or law firm may use internet web sites, professional cards, professional announcement cards, office signs, letterheads, or similar professional notices or devices, provided the same do not violate these Rules or any statute or court rule.
>
> (b) (1) A lawyer or law firm in private practice shall not practice under:
> (i) a false, deceptive, or misleading trade name;
> (ii) a false, deceptive, or misleading domain name; or
> (iii) a name that is misleading as to the identity of the lawyer or lawyers practicing under such name.
>
> (2) Specific Guidance Regarding Law Firm Names
> (i) Such terms as "legal aid," "legal service office," "legal assistance office," "defender office," and the like may be used only by bona fide legal assistance organizations.
> (ii) A law firm name, trade name, or domain name may not include the terms "non-profit" or "not-for-profit" unless the law firm qualifies for those designations under applicable law.
> (iii) A lawyer or law firm in private practice may not include the name of a nonlawyer in its firm name.
> (iv) The name of a professional corporation shall contain "PC" or such symbols permitted by law.
> (v) The name of a limited liability company or limited liability partnership shall contain "LLC," "LLP" or such symbols permitted by law.
> (vi) A lawyer or law firm may utilize a telephone number that contains a trade name, domain name, nickname, moniker, or motto that does not otherwise violate these Rules.

(Rule 7.5 has further paragraphs on matters such as lawyers holding public office and nonlawyer affiliations. Check the current NYSBA consolidated rules before advising on those edge cases.)

**Comment summaries (NYSBA, 2020):**
- **Cmt [1]:** Names, trade names, domain names, websites, social media pages, signs, cards, letterhead, and professional designations are all communications about the lawyer's services and must not be false, deceptive, or misleading.
- **Cmt [2]:** A firm may use the names of current members, or of retired or deceased members where there's a continuing line of succession. Trade names, domain names, distinctive website addresses, and social handles are allowed if not misleading.
- **Cmt [3]:** A name is misleading if it implies a connection with a government agency, a deceased or retired lawyer not in the firm's line of succession, a lawyer not associated with the firm, a nonlawyer, or a public or charitable legal services organization.
- **Cmt [4]:** Using the name of a lawyer holding public office is misleading during any substantial period when that lawyer isn't actively and regularly practicing with the firm.
- **Cmt [5]:** Lawyers may not imply they practice together as a firm, or as partners, when they don't. Don't hold out counsel, associate, or affiliate relationships that don't exist, and don't claim affiliation with other firms that isn't a fact.
- NYSBA Op. 1207 (2020): trade names such as acronyms or street names are allowed if not misleading. Whether a name is misleading is a heavily fact-specific question. Misleading names can also violate 8.4(c). Op. 1230 extends the principle to letterhead.

## 6. Rule 8.4(c): Misconduct

> A lawyer or law firm shall not: … (c) engage in conduct involving dishonesty, fraud, deceit or misrepresentation;

Deceptive marketing can be charged under 8.4(c) as well as 7.1. NYSBA notes that communications made to look like legal documents, though no longer separately banned, would likely violate both.

## 7. Rule 1.6(a): Confidential information (definition)

> "Confidential information" consists of information gained during or relating to the representation of a client, whatever its source, that is (a) protected by the attorney-client privilege, (b) likely to be embarrassing or detrimental to the client if disclosed, or (c) information that the client has requested be kept confidential. "Confidential information" does not ordinarily include (i) a lawyer's legal knowledge or legal research or (ii) information that is generally known in the local community or in the trade, field or profession to which the information relates.

**Marketing impact:** case studies, named clients, client logos, matter details, and **review replies** can disclose confidential information. Even confirming that someone was a client can be confidential. Information in a public court file is not automatically "generally known" (NYSBA cmt. [4A]). Get informed client consent in writing before naming clients or describing identifiable matters.

## 8. Rules 5.1 and 5.3: Supervision

Rule 5.3 makes lawyers responsible for nonlawyer conduct (including retained marketing agencies) that would violate the rules if a lawyer did it, when the lawyer orders or ratifies it, or when a partner or supervisor knew or should have known and failed to act. **Practical implication:** the agency's work is the firm's conduct, so attorney sign-off is not optional.

## 9. Judiciary Law (statutes, separate from the ethics rules)

> **§ 479.** It shall be unlawful for any person or his agent, employee or any person acting on his behalf, to solicit or procure through solicitation either directly or indirectly legal business, or to solicit or procure through solicitation a retainer, written or oral, or any agreement authorizing an attorney to perform or render legal services, or to make it a business so to solicit or procure such business, retainers or agreements.

> **§ 481.** It shall be unlawful for any person in the employ of or in any capacity attached to any hospital, sanitarium, police department, prison or court, or for a person authorized to furnish bail bonds, to communicate directly or indirectly with any attorney or person acting on his behalf for the purpose of aiding, assisting or abetting such attorney in the solicitation of legal business or the procurement through solicitation of a retainer, written or oral, or any agreement authorizing the attorney to perform or render legal services.

> **§ 482.** It shall be unlawful for an attorney to employ any person for the purpose of soliciting or aiding, assisting or abetting in the solicitation of legal business or the procurement through solicitation either directly or indirectly of a retainer, written or oral, or of any agreement authorizing the attorney to perform or render legal services.

**Why it matters:** these statutes were **not** changed by the 2026 rule amendments. A firm that hires callers, "lead runners", or appointment setters to solicit specific prospects can face Judiciary Law exposure even where an ethics-rule exception might arguably apply. Flag any outsourced outreach program for attorney review.

## 10. Outside the ethics rules

- **FTC Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 C.F.R. Part 465, effective October 2024):** prohibits fake or false reviews and testimonials (including AI-generated ones), buying positive or negative reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, and suppressing reviews through threats or false accusations. Conditioning incentives on a review's sentiment is prohibited.
- **Google Business Profile and Maps policies:** the business name must match the real-world name, with no keywords, locations, or taglines added. No incentives for reviews, no selective solicitation ("review gating"), no reviews by employees or owners. Self-serving review markup (aggregateRating on your own LegalService/Attorney schema) isn't eligible for review rich results and can mislead if not genuine.
- **NYSBA Op. 1207 and Op. 1230:** firm and trade names, and letterhead.
