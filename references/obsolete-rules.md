# Pre-June 2026 requirements that are NO LONGER mandatory

Most NY attorney-advertising guidance online (agency blogs, older CLE materials, AI training data) describes the rules that were in force from 2007 to May 31, 2026. Do **not** flag any of the following as required. You may recommend a few of them voluntarily (marked ✓ voluntary) where they reduce misleading-ness risk.

| Old requirement (former rule) | Status since June 1, 2026 | Voluntary? |
|---|---|---|
| "Attorney Advertising" label on website home page, brochures, and mailers (7.1(f)) | Eliminated | Harmless if kept; no need to add |
| "ATTORNEY ADVERTISING" in email subject lines (7.1(f)) | Eliminated | Not recommended (hurts deliverability) |
| "Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome" required with results, comparisons, testimonials, quality claims (7.1(d)–(e)) | Eliminated as a requirement | ✓ Recommended next to case results |
| Principal law office **address** in every ad (7.1(h)) | Replaced by 7.1(d): name + contact info of a responsible lawyer or firm (phone **or** address per Cmt [10]) | — |
| Pre-approval of ads and retention of copies for 3 years; website snapshots every 90 days; 1-year retention of computer-accessed ads (7.1(k)) | Eliminated | ✓ Keeping an archive is still smart evidence practice |
| No testimonials about pending matters; disclose paid endorsements (7.1(c)(1)–(2)) | Eliminated as specific rules; still governed by 7.1(a), the FTC rule, and Google policy | — |
| No portrayal of judges or fictitious firms; disclose actors and dramatizations (7.1(c)(3)–(4)) | Eliminated as specific rules; undisclosed dramatizations can still mislead | ✓ Disclose dramatizations |
| No attention-getting techniques "clearly unrelated to legal competence" (7.1(c)(5)) | Eliminated | — |
| No ads resembling legal documents (7.1(c)(6)) | Eliminated as a specific rule; still likely misleading and an 8.4(c) issue | — |
| No nickname, moniker, motto, or trade name implying ability to obtain results (7.1(c)(7)) | Eliminated from 7.1; still misleading under 7.1(a); vanity numbers are still limited by 7.5(b)(2)(vi) | — |
| No pop-up/pop-under ads except on own site; no meta tags that would violate the rules if displayed (7.1(g)) | Eliminated; hidden misleading content still violates 7.1(a) | — |
| Fee-advertising mechanics: scope statements, honoring fees for 30/90 days, range and hourly rate rules, Judiciary Law §488(3) cross-reference (7.1(j), (l)–(n), (p)) | Eliminated from the rule; fee claims must still be accurate and complete | — |
| No paying media for publicity in a news item (7.1(o)) | Eliminated as a specific rule; undisclosed paid placement is still misleading | — |
| Rule 7.4 limits on "specialist" or "specializing" claims | Moved to 7.1(c); only **certified** claims are restricted; "specialist" allowed if accurate (Cmt [7]) | — |
| "Advertisement" definition excluding existing clients and other lawyers (1.0(a)) | Deleted; 7.1 now covers **all** communications about services, including to clients and lawyers | — |
| "Computer-accessed communication" definition (1.0(c)) | Deleted | — |
| Filing solicitations with the disciplinary committee; keeping recipient lists for 3 years (old 7.3(c)) | Eliminated | — |
| 30-day bar on soliciting PI/wrongful-death victims (old 7.3(e)) | Eliminated (federal aviation/rail statutes and Judiciary Law §480 still apply) | — |
| "SAMPLE" / "DO NOT SIGN" marking on retainers included with solicitations (old 7.3(g)) | Eliminated | — |

**Still in force and NOT changed in 2026:** Rule 7.2 (payment for referrals), Rule 7.5 (names and letterhead, 2020 version), Rule 8.4(c), Rule 1.6, Rules 5.1/5.3, and Judiciary Law §§479–482.
